# `models/sauvc_pool` & `models/sauvc_gate` — SAUVC qualification arena

Simulation of the SAUVC qualification course used by this team. It lives in
its own world, `worlds/sauvc_qualification.world`; the three training worlds
are untouched.

| Model | Contents |
|---|---|
| `model://sauvc_pool` | pool floor (shallow shelf, slope, deep section) + 4 walls |
| `model://sauvc_gate` | the floating gate: 2 orange posts + 1 white top bar |

Both are `<static>true</static>`, so Buoyancy and Hydrodynamics only act on
the ROV. The gate is pinned at the water surface and does not bob.

## Coordinates

| Axis | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `x = 0` | pool edge / start line, the gate is at `x = 10` |
| `+x` | forward, into the pool |
| `+y` | left |
| `z = 0` | water surface |
| `+z` | up |

The pool is **10 m wide** (`y = -5 .. +5`). The SAUVC rules do not state a
width, so 10 m is a choice — change the `10` in every `size` and the `5.15`
in `wall_left` / `wall_right` to change it.

## Bottom profile

| Section | x range | Depth | Bottom z |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shallow shelf | 0.0 – 1.8 m | 1.28 m | −1.28 |
| Slope | 1.8 – 19.3 m | 1.28 → 2.00 m | −1.28 → −2.00 |
| Deep flat | 19.3 – 20.3 m | 2.00 m | −2.00 |

The slope drops 0.72 m over a 17.5 m run, i.e. ~2.4°. The model was checked
against this table: the top face of `floor_slope` starts at exactly
`(1.8, −1.28)` and ends at exactly `(19.3, −2.00)`.

## Pool surface (tiles)

The real pool is tiled with small rectangular tiles: whitish-cyan tiles with
dark blue grout on the floor, and blue tiles on the walls. The reference photo
was taken 2 m deep, facing the pool edge.

This workspace's Gazebo 8 has **no fog or underwater absorption**, so the
water colour cannot be applied by the renderer. Instead it is baked into the
tile textures. The result matches the photo closely:

| Surface | Rendered | Photo |
|---|---|---|
| floor tile | `#22B0D2` | `#20B0D0` |
| floor grout (near) | `#005C78` … `#006688` | `#005976` |
| wall tile | `#0153A2` | `#0050A0` |
| lane line | `#025496` | dark navy |

Tile geometry:

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| tile size | 20 cm × 10 cm (long side across the pool on the floor, horizontal on the walls) |
| grout | 4 mm |
| floor lane lines | 4 lines, 25 cm wide, at `y = ±1` and `y = ±3` (five 2 m sections; the gate sits in the middle section) |
| wall stripes | 25 cm wide on the pool-edge wall (`x = 0`), floor to surface, at the same four positions |

### How the visuals are built

Collisions are still plain boxes (invisible). The visible surface is three
meshes in `models/sauvc_pool/meshes/`:

| Mesh | Contents |
|---|---|
| `floor.obj` | shallow shelf + slope + deep section |
| `walls.obj` | the four walls |
| `markings.obj` | lane lines and wall stripes |

Their UVs are in **world metres**, so the 1 m tile texture repeats at the
correct physical size on every face, including the sloped floor, instead of
being stretched once across a whole box. That is why the tiles line up across
the floor/wall edges.

The floor slabs visually overlap the wall volumes by 5 cm (`VISUAL_OVERLAP`
in the generator). Without that, the end face of a floor box and the inner
face of a wall sit in the same plane and z-fight into stripes. Collisions are
unaffected and keep the exact sizes.

Generated by:

```bash
python3 scripts/make_pool_assets.py
```

which writes the textures and the three OBJ files. Change the tile size,
grout width or colours in the constants at the top of that script, then
re-run it.

> Note for anyone editing the materials: in this Gazebo build a `<pbr>`
> material that has **only** an `<albedo_map>` renders pure black. Every pool
> material therefore also carries the classic `<diffuse>` / `<specular>`
> pair, which is what the stock gz-sim worlds do. The walls additionally use
> `<emissive_map>` (`pool_tiles_wall_emissive.png`): they receive no direct
> sunlight, and the patterned glow keeps the grout visible instead of
> flattening the wall into one colour.

## Gate

| Part | Colour | Size |
|---|---|---|
| `post_left`, `post_right` | orange `#FF910F` | cylinder, 4 cm diameter, 100 cm long, vertical at `y = ±0.75` |
| `bar_top` | white `#FFFFFF` | cylinder, 4 cm diameter, 150 cm long, along `y` |

The upper side of the bar sits exactly at `z = 0`, so the gate floats with
its top on the water surface. The nominal opening is 1.50 m × 1.00 m measured
between post centres and from the surface; the clear opening is about
1.46 m × 0.96 m because the tubes are 4 cm thick.

At `x = 10` the bottom is 1.617 m deep, so there is about 0.62 m of clearance
under the gate.

### Colour fidelity

The colours matter because the vision pipeline keys on them. Gazebo expects
**linear** RGB, while the SAUVC reference colours are sRGB hex, so they were
converted:

| Reference | Linear RGB in SDF |
|---|---|
| `#FF910F` | `1.000 0.283 0.005` |
| `#FFFFFF` | `1 1 1` |

Vertical faces get no direct sunlight in this scene, so the tubes also carry
`<emissive>` (0.85 × the linear colour). Without it the posts render dark
brown. Measured from the `fpv_camera` image at 1.5 m:

| Part | Rendered | Target |
|---|---|---|
| posts | `#FE920F` | `#FF910F` |
| top bar | `#FEFFFF` | `#FFFFFF` |

If you change the scene lighting, re-check these numbers — the emissive
values were tuned for the current `<scene>` ambient of `0.45 0.50 0.55`.

## Running

```bash
cd ~/ROV-Simulation

# plain (TPP, same camera workflow as the other worlds)
./scripts/start_sim.sh sauvc_qualification.world

# with the FPV panel; the camera topic follows the world name automatically
./scripts/start_sim.sh sauvc_qualification.world --fpv
```

The ROV spawns at `(0.4, 0, −0.6)`, just inside the pool edge, facing the
gate. As in the other worlds the model is slightly positively buoyant, so it
drifts up when the thrusters are idle.

## Changing the course

* **Move the gate**: edit the `<pose>` of the `sauvc_gate` `<include>` in the
  world file (`10 0 0 0 0 0` = 10 m ahead, centred, parallel to the edge).
* **Change the depth profile**: edit the `<pose>` and `<size>` of
  `floor_shallow`, `floor_slope`, `floor_deep`. For the slope, remember the
  box centre is offset half its thickness along the local normal so that the
  **top** face lands on the profile. The header comment in
  `models/sauvc_pool/model.sdf` shows the maths.
* **Pool width**: see above.
* **Tiles / colours**: edit `scripts/make_pool_assets.py` and re-run it (see
  "Pool surface" above).
* **Lane lines**: `LANES_Y` and `LANE_W` in the same script.

## Validity

```bash
SDF_PATH=$HOME/ROV-Simulation/models gz sdf -k models/sauvc_pool/model.sdf
SDF_PATH=$HOME/ROV-Simulation/models gz sdf -k models/sauvc_gate/model.sdf
```

`gz sdf -k` on the world file fails while offline because the `Sun` include
comes from Fuel; the training worlds fail in exactly the same way.
